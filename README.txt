Native Tactical 2

This is a separate single-player mod folder based on Native. It does not replace Native.
Select "Native Tactical 2" in the Warband launcher. Start a new campaign to receive the Ashen Crown quest and initial five Crown Guards. Existing campaigns can still use the army and kingdom features; assign Crown Guards from Camp -> "Prepare your army" -> personal Crown Guard.

The new story, "The Ashen Crown", starts with a royal betrayal. Its quest log records the rebellion and completes when you become the leader of your own kingdom. This uses Warband's existing rebellion and kingdom systems.

How to start the story:
1. In the Warband launcher, select the module "Native Tactical 2" and launch the game.
2. Choose "New Campaign" and create your character. There is no separate quest menu to open: the quest "The Ashen Crown" starts automatically in script_game_start, you receive five Crown Guards, and the first quest note is written to your quest log.
3. Open the quest log (Q by default) to read the first note, then use the Camp menu to prepare your army and speak with companions and lords to advance the story. The quest finishes when you rebel and become the ruler of your own kingdom. Existing saves keep working, but the automatic quest start and the five starting guards only happen on a new campaign.

Open the Camp menu and choose "Prepare your army" to set an equipment preset, buy one copy of the selected uniform for your inventory, choose an automatic battle plan, assign a companion commander, select broad troop divisions, or set the exact number of each regular troop type to deploy. The roster screen includes a one-click reset that restores all available troop types to full deployment. Companions always deploy; reserved troops return after battle with their wounded status preserved. The uniform preset changes the player and Crown Guard battlefield equipment only, so regular troops and companions keep their authored loadouts and protection. You can also open these preparations from a hostile encounter before choosing to fight.

Camp -> "Army logistics" is a persistent field-supply system. Each day, the quartermaster consumes food based on party size, calculates supply days and readiness, and improves or damages party morale. When supplies run out, remaining food is consumed and the shortage continues to affect the army instead of leaving the inventory untouched. Shortages create a continuing readiness crisis instead of a cosmetic warning. The logistics screen can purchase an emergency supply train for 800 denars containing bread, grain, and cheese, or establish a 600-denar field hospital that heals the party's wounded before a long march.

Environmental survival: the daily logistics report also tracks seasonal weather stress. Winter and mountain snow reduce morale and, when morale collapses, can cause limited night desertions of regular soldiers; heroes and Crown Guards are protected. Mud-season pressure is milder but still compounds supply shortages. Repeated shortages, severe weather, and unburied losses create disease pressure with suspected, active, and severe stages. Active outbreaks remove a small number of ordinary soldiers and report the losses without touching companions or heroes. The logistics menu can establish a 400-denar quarantine camp (losing one day and suppressing infection), buy 500-denar medical supplies that reduce pressure over several days, organize 150-denar burials, or abandon the infected baggage train to cut contagion at the cost of all current supply days and readiness.

Dark Secrets & Extortion: while the spy network is active, the intelligence menu can spend 1,500 treasury denars to investigate a player vassal. Conspirators are easier to expose, but failed investigations alert the target and damage relations. Verified evidence has a strength and exposure-risk value and identifies treasonous correspondence, murder, smuggling, or a foreign pact. The player can blackmail the lord into changing faction, surrendering a walled center, sabotaging field-party morale, or preserving the secret for later. A failed leverage roll leaks the scandal, damages relations, and increases royal displeasure; evidence is consumed after the confrontation.

Mid-battle betrayal: at least twenty seconds into a real battle, the tactical layer evaluates allied kingdom lords who are present as agents. Conspiracy stage, discovered secrets, and personal relation with the player produce a betrayal score; only one betrayal can occur per battle. If the hidden roll succeeds, the turncoat lord switches to the enemy team, team relations become hostile, the player team receives an immediate fallback order, and a stark warning names the turncoat. The party morale penalty represents the shock, while the conspirator is marked as exposed for later political consequences. The event is intentionally rare and only affects eligible allied lords, not companions or ordinary troops; the lord-only conversion prevents the engine from accidentally converting the player's nearby formations.

After a betrayal, the kingdom stores a political tension value that starts at 25, raises royal displeasure, and temporarily damages the player's relations with uninvolved vassals. The tension decays by one point per day, preserving the political aftermath without creating a permanent campaign penalty.

The royal council can fund a 2,000-denar inquiry and reconciliation while tension remains. It reduces tension by 20, lowers royal displeasure, and restores a small amount of relation with surviving vassals; the action is unavailable once the crisis has naturally cooled.

Battle briefing: three seconds after a battle begins, the tactical layer scans allied lord agents and issues a warning for high-risk turncoats. The warning marks the lord as dangerous and raises coup alert, allowing the player to pull the Crown Guard closer before the twenty-second betrayal check.

Disease propagation: active or severe disease can now spread from the player's field army to nearby towns, castles, villages, and allied lord parties. Each recipient gains pressure gradually and advances through the same suspected, active, and severe stages; the spread is distance-limited and probabilistic so the entire map is not infected at once.

Turncoat judgment: after a battle betrayal, the exposed lord remains available in the Royal court. The player may arrest the lord and keep him as a prisoner, or execute him through a military tribunal. Arrest preserves the lord for later political handling; execution raises royal displeasure and permanently removes the lord from active service.

Turncoat detachments: when a betrayal fires, allied agents within 18 meters of the lord are converted with him. Agents in the lord's same tactical division can also be converted out to 30 meters, making the bodyguard and immediate detachment reliable while avoiding conversion of the entire player army.

Battlefield weather presentation: the stored environmental stress now affects battle presentation. Severe stress produces snow, dense fog, and the native rain ambience; moderate stress produces rain, fog, and the same ambience; normal conditions clear rain and fog. A gong warning accompanies a confirmed turncoat event.

Deterministic resolution: disease propagation now occurs whenever the documented distance and infection-stage conditions are met, without a hidden roll. Secret discovery is based on conspiracy stage and target relation; evidence strength, risk, and secret category are derived from those facts. Extortion leaks only when the evidence-risk threshold is objectively high, and mid-battle betrayal requires a score of at least 60. This keeps outcomes explainable and reproducible; unrelated Native world-generation systems still retain their original randomness.

The module build script now invokes the installed Python 2.7 interpreter explicitly and stops with a clear error if that interpreter is missing. This avoids silently running the Module System with an incompatible Python version.

The Ashen Crown quest now offers a one-time choice: protect the villages (improve village relations and set 75% taxes) or prepare a war chest (receive 2,000 denars and set 125% taxes). Recruit three companions to advance the story, then rebel and become ruler of your own kingdom to finish it. You can continue playing after the quest.

Royal Guard mounts: Crown Guards, Crown Guard Veterans, and Royal Champions are guaranteed elite chargers in their troop templates, so Warband spawns them mounted reliably. Surrender now leads to explicit field or castle captivity text instead of the old placeholder message.
The guard mount check runs again two seconds after mission start, after the player agent and all reinforcements have spawned, so the guard formation follows the player's actual mount state rather than spawn order.
Guard troop templates now receive the mounted flag, guaranteed horse flag, Riding 4, and an elite Charger directly in their inventory. This avoids unreliable post-spawn mounting and makes the mounted spawn deterministic.

Unit movement: every order you give is executed by the whole block. Select a division with the normal number keys, open the battle orders map with Backspace, and click a destination: the block marches to that point in formation. F5 re-centres the block on your current position. In the order panel, Advance pushes the block 12 m forward along its facing, Fall back pulls it 12 m back, Charge drops the block anchor on the closest enemy so the line advances into contact, Follow re-centres the block on you, and Hold freezes it where it stands. The ranks are re-locked after every order, so the army keeps its shape the whole time. Native F1/F2/F3 orders keep their normal game behaviour, and the tactical layer watches the team's movement order so that charge, advance, fall back, follow and hold also move the block instead of being ignored. The automatic plan is paused so it cannot overwrite your order. Allied human agents use a class-based speed modifier instead of Native's default: cavalry 140%, mounted archers 135%, infantry 125%, foot archers and Crown Guard 120%, plus 20% while marching to a slot. A living captain's aura raises nearby allies to 185%, panic drops them to 90%, and a morale-broken army is held at 110%.

Strict marching formation: at battle start every allied human agent receives a formation group and a fixed slot inside one block centred on the player. The block is layered front to back as cavalry, infantry, mounted archers, and foot archers, with the Crown Guard kept as the rear reserve. Mounted troops carrying a bow, crossbow, or throwing weapon are classified as mounted archers and receive their own layer instead of mixing with shock cavalry. Each layer is up to five men wide, soldiers stand 1.8 m apart, and the distance between the last rank of one layer and the first rank of the next is exactly 3 m. The block is anchored on the player's position and facing at the moment the battle begins and does not drift afterwards: soldiers are held on their slots by scripted movement, so they neither advance on their own nor walk back and forth. The line stays exactly where it was deployed until you give an order, and every order moves the block as a whole instead of breaking the ranks. Formation maintenance runs every 400 ms. F6 re-forms the block where the men are standing and rallies a broken army, F7 brings every man (including a detached Crown Guard) back around you, and F8 releases the formation completely.

Battle plans are now real manoeuvres executed by the whole block, and the interval between soldiers changes with the plan: Shield Wall, Hedgehog and Assault Column close to 1.2 m, Skirmish opens to 2.6 m, and every other plan keeps 1.8 m. Defend, Shield Wall, Hedgehog, Archer Volley, Counterattack and Envelopment square the block up and hold. Advance and Assault Column keep the block marching at roughly 1.5 m per second until the enemy is about 30 m from the block centre. Cavalry Flank and Envelopment wheel the block out to the right and then roll it up the enemy's line. Skirmish keeps the block between roughly 30 m and 45 m, giving ground when the enemy closes and following when he falls back. Archer Volley orders the archers and mounted archers to hold fire until the enemy is inside 40 m. Counterattack holds the line and only charges when the enemy is inside 22 m. A plan never breaks the ranks and never turns the army into a permanent player-follow group; any manual order cancels the plan's march immediately.

Battle size is fixed at 205 total agents through module.ini (`battle_size_min = 205` and `battle_size_max = 205`): up to 200 regular battle slots plus five mandatory Crown Guard slots. The actual split between the two sides still follows Warband's battle advantage calculation.

Every standard battle also forces the player's Battle Size slider to its maximum before agents spawn. This ensures the engine uses the 205-agent cap instead of a lower value saved in the player's options. A battle can still contain fewer agents when the participating parties do not collectively have enough fit soldiers.

The five Crown Guards are always kept in the active battle roster and are never moved to the tactical reserve by the deployment settings. They occupy the additional five slots, so disabling ordinary troop categories cannot remove them.

Formation orders: the army is deployed into the layered block when the battle begins and holds that ground. Every order is then executed by the whole block - an order-panel button, the Backspace movement map, F5, or a battle plan. Advance, Fall back, Charge, Follow and Hold move the block anchor, the ranks keep their slots, and the soldiers march and fight together, so the formation is never left behind and the troops never snap back. This is also why an attack order no longer dissolves the line: the block advances into contact as one body. F6 re-forms the block where the men are standing, F7 brings the whole army (including a detached Crown Guard) back around you, and F8 cancels the formation entirely so the troops fight under Native orders (press F8 again, or F7, to reform). Delegating the lead to a companion commander does not cancel the block: only you can release the ranks.

Whole-army formation control: press 0 for Warband's normal all-units selection when issuing a standard order. Press F7 to return every allied unit to the marching block at once: cavalry in front, infantry behind them, mounted archers behind the infantry, foot archers behind them, and Crown Guard at the rear.

The Ashen Crown is now a linear fourteen-chapter campaign rather than a sandbox completion path. Founding a kingdom no longer completes the quest. After the rebellion, the player must stabilize the realm with 5,000 treasury denars and three loyal vassals, win twelve campaign battles in the final war, reach 1,000 renown, and then choose the final mercy-or-iron judgment. The Camp story-path menu always displays the active chapter and its exact objective, and the quest is completed only after that final judgment.

After establishing your own active kingdom, use Camp -> "Royal council" to appoint or replace a minister, hold a marshal election, review the weekly budget, set low/standard/high taxes, manage a royal treasury, issue ministerial orders, and read the minister's report. Treasury income is generated weekly from a 15% levy on the accumulated rents and tariffs of kingdom-held towns and castles, scaled by the selected tax policy, plus daily income from active trade agreements. The weekly settlement estimates wages for the player's field army, vassal armies, and realm garrisons, pays as much as the treasury can afford, and records the actual payroll and any shortfall alongside minister orders and withdrawals. An unpaid payroll shortfall damages vassal relations. Low taxes improve vassal relations over time; high taxes reduce them, while an appointed minister improves relations monthly. The report shows the live counts of loyal, neutral, and discontented vassals, average personal relation, treasury, revenues, expenses, and payroll. You can deposit or withdraw 1,000, 5,000, or 10,000 denars. Public works cost 3,000 treasury denars and improve prosperity across realm holdings. Army training costs 2,500 treasury denars, improves party morale, and trains garrison troops. Select a town, castle, or village controlled by your kingdom to invest 1,500 treasury denars or recruit a garrison wave for 4,000 treasury denars (towns and castles only). Tax rates also affect accumulated fief rents and town tariffs at weekly settlement.

The council now includes internal votes on military campaigns and tax policy, plus a persistent meeting record showing the last summit, decision, and invasion-front status. The Black Fleet invasion expands deterministically every 20 days during the crisis and can seize additional walled centers instead of using only fixed beachheads. The Ashen Crown epilogue is locked until Chapter XIV; the world-war victory and defeat routes display distinct cinematic outcome messages and cannot be selected early.

Camp -> "Mercenary contracts and free companies" (also reachable from the minister's royal report) opens the mercenary ledger and its recruitment screens. While you hold no crown or oath, you can sign a 30-day contract with any of the six kingdoms. The weekly wage is fixed at signing and scales with renown, rank (Free Lance, Sworn Blade, War Captain, Legend of the Free Companies), completed contracts, and the employer's active wars. Every seven days the wage is paid from the employer's war chest; an empty chest means no pay and a relation loss. The ledger shows employer, rank, wage, days remaining, next payment, completed contracts, relation and blacklist, with direct Renew (+14 days, up to 60) and Break buttons. Breaking a contract costs 8 relation (12 during the first week), improves relations with the employer's enemies, and blocks all offers for 30 days; honouring a contract to its end grants +3 relation and raises future wages. The lower half of the ledger lists four free companies - Iron Ravens, Blackwood Archers, Crimson Lancers and Steel Legion - with captain, troop count, 14-day and permanent fees, renown requirement and relation. Hire with one click; captain relation grows with each contract, or through a 500-denar gift only while that company is actively serving you, and lowers later fees by up to 25%. Temporary companies leave when their 14 days expire, permanent retainers stay until you dismiss them from the company screen. An NPC employer's war chest starts at 12,000 denars because this mod does not simulate independent kingdom budgets.

All six native kingdoms and any active player-founded kingdom hold a kingdom election every 90 campaign days. Election schedules are stored per faction and begin 90 days after the feature first initializes in a campaign. The player receives a one-time warning 15 days before the next election; Camp -> "Kingdom election calendar and your rank" shows the exact days remaining. At the election, the player may stand or decline if eligible. Each eligible AI lord independently opts in or out using a randomized decision influenced by renown, leadership, and their relation with the incumbent; AI lords vote using personal relations, candidate renown, leadership skill, field-army size, and an incumbent bonus. Ties favor the incumbent. The winner becomes faction leader; voters' relations with the winner improve by 5 if they supported the winner or fall by 3 otherwise. A defeated player ruler remains in the realm as a vassal, and a player elected to a native kingdom becomes its ruler. Elections are resolved automatically for kingdoms the player does not belong to.

Player vassals and rulers earn permanent ranks as renown rises: Knight at 300, Noble at 600, Kingdom Champion at 1,000, and Realm Regent at 1,500. Promotions improve relations with the kingdom's lords, add 5 troops per rank to the player's party limit, and unlock a daily treasury allowance of 25 denars per rank while the player remains in an active kingdom and the treasury can afford it. Rank also adds 25 election-score points per level when the player stands for the crown. The Camp election calendar shows current rank and benefits.

As monarch, open Camp -> Royal council -> "Issue royal decrees" to declare war or make peace directly with an eligible kingdom, set tax policy, reassign any kingdom-held town/castle/village to a current vassal, or exile a lord whose relation is -50 or lower. Exile strips the lord's holdings using Native's treason procedure and cannot be undone. These decrees do not require a minister; honor awards, spy operations, and other minister services retain their existing requirements.

Crown Guards now progress through three ranks: Crown Guard, Crown Guard Veteran, and Royal Champion. Deployed surviving guards earn 250 experience after a battle victory and can be promoted through the party upgrade screen; reserved guards do not gain combat experience from battles they did not enter. Veteran ranks use stronger default equipment and skills. With a minister appointed, order additional training for 2,500 treasury denars to grant assigned guards 600 experience each.

Discontented vassals can form a covert conspiracy. When a vassal's relation falls to -50 or lower, a daily process tracks secret contact with a rival kingdom; the next stage can siphon up to 5% of the treasury (minimum 100, maximum 1,500 denars), and a later stage prepares treason. Improving relation above -35 stops the plot. A minister can investigate for 1,000 denars, which exposes active conspirators in the vassal report. You can grant an exposed or suspected vassal amnesty for 2,500 denars (+15 relation), or prosecute an exposed traitor using Native's treason and exile consequences. Player-kingdom defections at -70 relation now require the conspiracy to reach its treason stage.

The minister can establish a seven-day spy network for 5,000 treasury denars. While active, reports for rival kingdoms show active lord parties, their troop counts and approximate distance from the player, plus the number and relations of discontented vassals. Reports can uncover a domestic plot if its secret contact points to that kingdom.

Use "Award royal honors" in the council to honor a current vassal or a companion in your party. The Order of the Crown, Royal Banneret, and Crown Champion cost 2,000, 5,000, and 10,000 treasury denars; each raises relation and party morale. The same menu lets the monarch reassign any kingdom-held center to a current vassal, improving relation by 15. The native fief-transfer procedure updates the center's ownership and banner.

The council can hold a summit with any number of selected active kingdoms and negotiates with each ruler in turn. Choose peace, trade, alliance, non-aggression, or relations as the agenda. Each ruler accepts, refuses, or makes up to six counteroffers using a score based on faction relations, the ruler's personal relation and reputation, the war balance, proposed payments, and (for alliances) common enemies. You can accept a counteroffer, revise your terms, or end talks; an offer too weak to merit a counterproposal is rejected outright. Peace terms range from a ceasefire to payments of 3,000-30,000 denars or reparations demands of 1,000 or 3,000 denars. Standard trade costs 2,500 denars for 30 days at 150 denars per peaceful day; revised terms and counteroffers range from 3,500 to 15,000 denars for the same duration and income. The council can propose a 30-day military alliance or a 45-day non-aggression pact; revised terms extend them up to 180 and 240 days respectively, while ruler counteroffers range from 5-25 days for an alliance and 15-40 days for non-aggression. Relations can be settled for a 5-point improvement; revised terms and counteroffers request 1,000-12,500 denars for a 10-35-point improvement. Agreement fees are paid from the royal treasury, and agreements are applied only after the treasury can cover them. Trade renewals also need ruler acceptance, and the term can be extended up to 90 days. Treaties can be reviewed; alliance and trade renewals are negotiated and agreements can be cancelled, while non-aggression uses Warband's truce timer. During a hostile field encounter, the option to call support appears when an allied lord is nearby and at war with the enemy; the lord joins that battle. The alliance ends after 30 days or if war breaks out between you. These political features are available in Sandbox campaigns once you rule your own kingdom, even without the Ashen Crown quest.

Before battle, select "Let my companion command; watch the next battle" to have the chosen commander lead while you observe. The army-preparation, troop-selection, guard, and commander menus have a "Back" option at the top. While spectating, press W to cycle the camera through living allies. Open the battle orders panel (Backspace) and select "Return to battle" to resume control. The observer is hidden and protected from damage while watching.

Battle hotkeys:
- 4: select only the Crown Guard division. It never gives an order by itself; use the normal order keys afterwards.
- F4: open the Tactical Plans popup. This only shows the plan buttons and does not execute anything until you click a plan. Press Escape to close it.
- F1, F2, F3 and every other Native key keep their original behaviour.
- Backspace: open the normal battle orders panel.

During a battle, open the battle orders panel (Backspace). Use the one-click Defend, Advance, Cavalry flank, Archer volley, and Regroup buttons to apply a plan immediately. Every plan is executed by the block: Defend, Archer volley, Shield Wall, Hedgehog, Counterattack and Envelopment hold it, Advance and Assault Column march it forward until contact, Cavalry flank takes it round the enemy's right, Skirmish keeps it at shooting range, and Regroup rebuilds it around you. Manual orders pause the automatic plan. Close ranks and Spread out change the real interval between soldiers: the block tightens from 1.8 m down to 1.0 m or opens up to 3.0 m and re-forms on the new spacing. The bottom-left of the panel shows a short key hint, the live Crown Guard order state, the current plan state, and a small Close (Esc) button. The panel refreshes the battle map and troop counts once per second.

Plans (all of them keep the layered block intact):
- Defend: the block holds its ground and waits, archers fire at will.
- Advance: the block squares up and then marches on the enemy at a steady pace, halting about 30 m from him.
- Cavalry Flank: the block wheels right and then advances on the enemy's flank in the same ranks.
- Archer Volley: the block holds and the archers and mounted archers keep their arrows until the enemy is inside 40 m.
- Regroup: the whole army reforms the layered block around the player.
- Shield Wall: closed ranks (1.2 m), hold the ground.
- Assault Column: closed ranks, then the same steady march as Advance.
- Hedgehog: closed ranks, hold with all-round readiness.
- Counterattack: hold the line and charge only when the enemy is inside 22 m.
- Envelopment: hold, then at 30 m wheel out to the right and take the enemy's flank.
- Skirmish: open ranks (2.6 m), keep 30-45 m from the enemy and shoot; the block gives ground when he closes.

Equipment presets replace head, body, foot, and hand armor for the player and Crown Guards in battle. Regular troops and companions retain their authored equipment. The separate inventory purchase adds one copy of each selected uniform piece to your inventory at normal item value. Choose 0-5 Crown Guards and give them follow, hold, or charge orders. Follow keeps them bolted to the rear of the block; Hold or Charge detaches them so they can stand as a stationary reserve or be committed on their own, and F7 brings them back into the ranks. They use their own Crown Guard division: in battle, the single key 4 selects that division and nothing else, and companions remain with the normal troops. The selected guard order is applied once at battle start so you can take manual control afterward. Five Crown Guards are added when a new campaign starts.

Limitations: troop selection is by troop type; soldiers of the same type cannot be individually distinguished. Allied support is available for field encounters, not siege assaults. The companion commander cannot be directly controlled. Negotiation is still structured and rule-based rather than free-form dialogue. The weekly royal payroll covers the player's field army, vassal armies, and realm garrisons using estimated Native wages; any shortfall is reported, and vassal relations reflect underfunding. The realm treasury does not replace Native's decentralized lord/fief budgets or account for every AI expense. Vassal payroll credits enter each lord's Native wealth budget, which also funds reinforcements, rather than being an earmarked wage payment. Conspiracies use relation thresholds and staged daily checks rather than a full simulation of personal secret networks; spy reports show current party counts and approximate map distance, not predicted future routes. Honors improve relations and morale but do not directly increase troop attributes. Low loyalty triggers warnings at -40 and -60 relation; a player-kingdom lord defects without a random chance only after a conspiracy reaches its treason stage, relation is -70 or lower, an active alternative kingdom exists, and the lord can intrigue. The minister can issue a 2,500-denar reconciliation grant to improve a discontented vassal's relation by 10. Spectator mode cycles through allies rather than offering a free-flying camera. Mercenary contracts are formal 30-day terms rather than free-form negotiations; NPC kingdom treasuries are abstract war chests rather than simulated economies; free companies rehire at full strength even if some of their troops died in your service, and dismissing a company removes its stored troops by type. The module builds successfully, but the new features, including the mercenary system, have not yet been verified in a live game session.

Major crises begin after day 180, when one kingdom controls at least 60% of all centers, or after 30 consecutive days of high taxes under a player monarch. Each crisis creates four raider or rebel parties, adding 100 troops to each party's template, and lasts up to 60 days; wars between active kingdoms are suspended at the outbreak. The player can lead the defense, remain neutral, or, as monarch, choose one opportunistic war. Defeating all crisis armies ends the emergency. A player who chose defense and won a battle against a crisis army receives 15,000 denars, 200 renown, and +10 relation with the player's vassals. An active crisis can be reviewed from Camp. Crisis parties currently spawn at villages rather than dedicated coastal landing zones; emergency truces do not create military alliances. The crisis feature builds successfully but still needs a live-campaign playtest.


The Ashen Crown: full story path (11 chapters)
The quest is no longer a short choice. It now follows a clear route that you can track at any time from Camp -> "The Ashen Crown: story path." and review in "The Ashen Crown: chronicle." The current chapter, its goal and your progress (battles won) are shown there, and the quest log records every milestone.

Chapter I - A House in Ashes: choose your path (protect the common folk or build a war chest).
Chapter II - Sworn Blades: gather three companions to your banner.
Chapter III - The Court of Shadows: earn a lord's trust (relation 10+) or swear homage.
Chapter IV - The Spymaster's Trail: pay the spymaster 1,500 denars for the traitor's name.
Chapter V - The Broken Banner: win three battles.
Chapter VI - The Loyalty of Lords: win three lords (relation 20+) or reach 400 renown.
Chapter VII - The Royal Vanguard: win eight battles and reach 600 renown.
Chapter VIII - The Council of Ashes: hold the war council (2,000 denars).
Chapter IX - Raise the Banner: raise your own faction.
Chapter X - The Crown at Last: become ruler of a kingdom.
Chapter XI - Epilogue: Mercy or Iron: choose mercy or iron and claim the crown.

Each completed chapter grants renown, gold and a quest note, so the road to the throne stays visible and rewarding.


Unique Unit Captains: Camp -> "Captain roster" tracks infantry/archer/cavalry counts and awards a unique captain (renown + gold) for every 100 soldiers in a class. See EPIC_SYSTEMS_DESIGN.md for the full epic storyline and captain system roadmap.


Rebellion & Coup: when the king's displeasure reaches 60, Camp -> "Rebellion: challenge the king" unlocks. Declare rebellion to raise your own faction and become its ruler; complete the coup to triumph. Manage displeasure via Camp -> "Royal court: audience with the king."


Captain Aura & Morale: in battle, living Unique Captains grant a speed buff to nearby allies within 15 m (185% movement speed); if a captain falls, nearby allies suffer a temporary panic (90% speed) and a message announces the fall. Protect your captains and target enemy captains.

Red Banquet & secret coup plots: when multiple hostile vassals reach the existing conspiracy stages, the court tracks hidden support and secret meetings. An active spy network produces deliberately vague tavern and sealed-letter reports every few days. Royal Council -> Intelligence Network -> Red Banquet lists suspected vassals rather than presenting certainty. Summoning a suspect opens an interactive banquet with three strategic choices: expose and prosecute the lord (raising public tension), continue the feast under surveillance (allowing a counterplot), or test loyalty on the battlefield. The system stores each kingdom's alert, banquet, tension, and last-report state, and reuses the existing Native treason procedure for prosecution.

Assassins & night raids: hostile kingdoms with relations at -60 or lower can dispatch an assassin after a hidden cooldown. Camp -> Rest under guard until dawn opens a midnight event when an attempt is active; draw your weapon, call Crown Guards, escape, or wait in ambush. Survival uses agility, strength, athletics, persuasion, alertness, and assigned Crown Guards. Failure wounds the player and damages party morale rather than forcing an arbitrary campaign death. With an active spy network, the royal intelligence screen also offers 8,000-denar retaliation contracts against hostile kingdoms. Contracts resolve after seven days as success, failure, or exposure; exposure costs honor, diplomatic relations, and public tension.

Dynamic Companions & marriage politics: companions who remain in service for 45 days may demand a future holding and greater command. The camp's companion politics screen lets you promise a path forward or refuse; ignoring the demand for a further eight days makes the companion leave the party and join the outlaws. A player's first marriage also grants a one-time diplomatic relation improvement with the spouse's original realm and 50 renown, representing a political claim.

Battle morale: the army breaks when its commander falls while the fight is already even or losing, when the living enemy outnumbers it two to one (and there are at least eight of them), or when it has lost sixty per cent of its starting strength while still the weaker side. A break is not cosmetic: the block is dissolved, every friendly human agent is released to Native AI, the soldiers scatter and the speed modifier drops to 110%. A winning army does not run because one man went down. The event is one-time per battle; F6 or F7 rallies the men and restores the ranks. It does not override Native victory or retreat rules.

Ranks close after casualties: every two seconds the block counts its living men. When the count drops, the survivors are renumbered inside their layer, the layer counts and the depth of every band are rebuilt, and the whole block dresses on the new slots. A hole left by a dead man no longer stays open or pushes the rear ranks out of position. A detached Crown Guard is included in the count but is not pulled back into the block.

Broken ground: the block can no longer wedge itself against a wall, a fence or a steep slope. While the formation is marching or has just been ordered to move, a check runs every two seconds. If at least two men who should be walking to their slots are more than 20 m from those slots and have moved less than one metre in two seconds, the march is cancelled. The anchor moves to where the men actually stand, the block steps 2.5 m to the side, and the ranks dress again. A five-second cooldown prevents the same obstacle from causing repeated halts.

Enemy battle plan (field battles only): after the first six seconds, the opposing army picks one stance from its division mix and the balance of strength, using ordinary Native team orders rather than scripting enemy agents. A clearly weaker enemy takes ground, holds the line and lets his archers fire at will. An enemy with at least a third of his men mounted and at least three riders sends his cavalry in first while the infantry advances. Any other enemy advances on the front. When the player's block comes within 25 m, or the enemy falls below 55% of his starting strength, the whole enemy line commits to the charge. A purely waiting enemy loses patience after 60 seconds and advances, so a field battle cannot stall forever. The enemy plan is attached to lead_charge, village_attack_bandits and village_raid only; siege battles are unchanged.

Ashen Crown World War: after Chapter XIV, the deterministic Chapter XV Black Fleet invasion seizes beachheads and creates a defensive coalition. Defeating it opens Chapter XVI, a 180-day world war requiring ten campaign victories. Victory makes the player the overlord of all active kingdoms and restores all centers to the player's realm; failure records the hero as captured in Chapter XVII.

Tactical command (settings, field manual, saved formations, battle report)

Camp -> "Tactical command" (or Army preparations -> "Tactical command") opens four screens: Settings, Field manual, Saved formations and the last battle report.

Settings: the formation system can be switched off, morale can be switched off, battle fatigue can be switched off, the command delay cycles through 0, 1, 2 and 3 seconds, and battle speed cycles through 75%, 100%, 125% and 150%. Defaults are written once for a new campaign and stay with the save.

Field manual: the battle keys, what each of the eleven plans does, how the three saved formations behave, and what every setting changes.

Saved formations: Box vs Cavalry (Hedgehog with closed ranks, F9), Arrow Storm (Archer Volley with open ranks, F10) and Hammer (Counterattack with normal ranks, F11). Choosing one in the menu makes it the automatic plan for the next battle; pressing F9, F10 or F11 in battle applies it immediately.

Compact HUD: while a battle runs, the top-right corner shows the current plan, the rank interval, the morale percentage and the living enemy and allied counts. It is a read-only overlay: it never takes a key, never blocks an order and disappears with the battle.

Battle report: when the battle is won, the game holds on a report screen before the mission ends. It lists our dead, wounded and missing, the enemy dead and wounded, prisoners, estimated loot, the formation that was used with its spacing, and a performance rating. Continue or Escape closes it and ends the battle. The same report stays available from Tactical command -> Last battle report.
